Erdős problem 1007 · uniqueness half

Nine edges at dimension four means K₃,₃

Place the vertices of a finite simple graph as distinct points so that every edge has length 1. The dimension is the smallest Euclidean dimension in which that is possible. If the dimension is 4, there are nine edges, and every vertex has a neighbour, the graph is K₃,₃.

Definitions

The three words in the sentence

Each one is doing work. Drop it, and the sentence becomes false or empty.

Length 1 on edges

A placement sends every edge to a unit segment. A non-edge may have length 1 as well. Here the edge is 0–1, and the non-edge 1–2 is also a unit segment.

Dimension means least

K₂ embeds in ℝ⁴. Its dimension is still 1, because a smaller placement exists. Dimension 4 asks for a graph that fits in ℝ⁴ and in no smaller Euclidean space.

Every vertex has a neighbour

Add an isolated vertex to K₃,₃. The dimension stays 4 and the edge count stays nine. The graph is no longer K₃,₃, so the hypothesis is part of the theorem.

The witness

K₃,₃ has a placement in ℝ⁴

Let r = 1/√2, so r² = 1/2. One part lies in the first coordinate plane, the other in the orthogonal plane. Each of the nine cross pairs differs in exactly two coordinates, by ±r, and the squared length is r² + r² = 1.

Both the ℝ⁴ placement and the ℝ³ obstruction are already kernel-checked. Padding a placement from ℝⁿ into ℝᵐ for n ≤ m is the lemma a worker is writing now. Until that lemma and the packaging are in place, “dimension exactly 4” is not yet a Lean theorem, so the witness that the statement is non-vacuous is still open.

The lower bound

Three centres in ℝ³ have at most two common unit points

Each vertex of the second part would have to lie at distance 1 from all three vertices of the first. Three distinct points cannot do that.

The six-vertex dead end

The other nine-edge graph lives in the plane

On six vertices, minimum degree 3 and nine edges force the graph to be 3-regular, so the complement is 2-regular. A 2-regular graph on six vertices is a 6-cycle or two disjoint triangles. The cycle’s complement is the triangular prism, and the prism has this unit-distance drawing in ℝ².

Re-attachment

A vertex of degree at most two can be put back in ℝ³

Delete u. When the degree is two, the hypothesis already places the two neighbours at distance 1. The deleted graph embeds in ℝ³, and u has infinitely many legal positions, so one of them avoids the finitely many points already used.

Degree 0

Any point outside the finite image. An isolated vertex is this case; the extremal graph forbids isolates, and the lemma still includes them.

Degree 1

The unit sphere about the unique neighbour. The drawing is a slice. Any unused point of the sphere realises the one edge.

Degree 2, one position

Neighbours at distance 1. This u completes an equilateral triangle, so both new edges have length 1. The height is √3/2.

  1. 1 Placements pass to subgraphs

    A unit-distance placement of G restricts to any subgraph, and pulls back along a graph embedding. The inequality of dimensions is not yet packaged as its own theorem.

  2. 2 Extra coordinates can be zero

    A placement in ℝⁿ appends zeros to give a placement in ℝᵐ whenever n ≤ m. This is what promotes “no placement in ℝ³” to “no placement below dimension 4”.

  3. 3 Put a low-degree vertex back

    The three pictures above. Degree 0 uses a free point, degree 1 the unit sphere, degree 2 the circle of radius √3/2.

  4. 4 Eight edges fit in ℝ³

    Take a finite graph with at most eight edges that does not embed in ℝ³, with as few edges and vertices as possible. A vertex of degree at most 2 would let step 3 rebuild it, so every degree is at least 3. Then 3|V| ≤ 2|E| ≤ 16, so there are four or five vertices, and the graph is a subgraph of K₅ minus an edge.

  5. 5 The extremal graph has minimum degree 3

    If a vertex of a nine-edge graph has degree 1 or 2, the graph produced by deleting it has at most eight edges. Step 4 places that graph in ℝ³, and step 3 puts the vertex back, so the original graph embeds in ℝ³.

  6. 6 Only five or six vertices remain

  7. 7 Five vertices is K₅ minus one edge

    Five vertices determine ten possible edges. A simple graph with nine edges omits exactly one of them, so it is K₅ minus an edge. The two endpoints of the missing edge have degree 3 and the other three vertices have degree 4.

    K₅ minus an edge already has a unit-distance placement in ℝ³, so this graph embeds in ℝ³. Its dimension is at most 3.

    The paper asserts the degree sequence (4,4,4,3,3) and gives no classification. The count of ten possible edges is the classification.

  8. 8 Six vertices forces K₃,₃

    Degree sum 18 and minimum degree 3 make every degree exactly 3. The complement is 2-regular. That dichotomy is kernel-checked: a 6-cycle, or two disjoint triangles.

    The graph is the prism drawn above. It embeds in ℝ², so its dimension is at most 2.

    the remaining graph

    Complement is two triangles

    Every vertex of one triangle is adjacent to every vertex of the other, and to nothing in its own triangle. That graph is K₃,₃.

  9. 9 Assemble the theorem, and check it is not empty

    Steps 5 through 8 leave only K₃,₃. Separately, K₃,₃ itself has nine edges, no isolated vertex, and dimension 4 once the two placements and the padding lemma are packaged. That witness is what stops the universal sentence from being vacuously true.
